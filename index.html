Jane Oliver

I study how machines learn to recognize — and standardize — a face, a body, a way of relating. My work sits at the intersection of feminist media theory, algorithmic culture, and political aesthetics: tracing how platforms and generative AI don't just reflect human life, but actively manufacture it — from beauty standards to the companions young people are learning to confide in.

About

I'm a researcher working at the intersection of critical digital humanities and AI governance. This summer I'm an AI Workstreams Research Intern at Harvard's Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society, contributing to the AI and Human Experience initiative — researching how young people form relationships with AI companion systems, what those relationships mean for socio-emotional development and adult relationship formation, and how design and policy should respond.

I just finished an MPhil in Digital Humanities at the University of Cambridge, where my dissertation examined how far-right movements use online beauty aesthetics and looksmaxxing culture as a vector for ideological recruitment and platform evasion. Before that, I studied English Language, Media & Communications at the University of Rochester, graduating with Honors and a Political Science minor.

My method draws on feminist media theory (Haraway, Federici, Wolf, Kristeva, Creed) and platform/visual culture critique (Steyerl, Sekula, Crawford & Paglen, Zylinska) to read AI systems not as neutral infrastructure, but as aesthetic and ideological actors. I'm currently weighing a JD and a PhD, with an eye toward staying in the research-policy space long term — somewhere that lets me keep writing, researching, and arguing about how technology governs bodies and culture.

Projects

Tools built at the Berkman Klein Center this summer, alongside the writing. Two are live and open to use; the third is in development.

Live · open tool

Companion AI Policy Tracker

Berkman Klein Center, 2026 · built and maintained by me

Forty-four live instruments across US federal, US state, EU, and Chinese law — sixteen states with an enacted chatbot or companion statute, and China's framework in force since 15 July 2026. Unlike existing trackers, which answer what has passed, this one codes the definitional anatomy of each instrument: what it calls the object, whether its test turns on capability, behaviour, purpose or training objective, which device narrows its reach, and whether the text actually covers general-purpose assistants. Released openly so the coding can be checked.

→ Open the tracker
why Fifty-odd instruments in four jurisdictions, most summarised somewhere and verified almost nowhere.

The Companion AI Quiz

Berkman Klein Center, 2026

A short quiz on how you actually use AI — not how you'd describe it. It places you on a companion index against the four functions, and hands back a prosocial skill set you can paste straight into your own chatbot. Built as the participatory half of the research: the definitional argument is easier to feel than to read.

Research

Working paper · draft complete, in review

What Is an "AI Companion"? The Definitional Problem

Berkman Klein Center, 2026 · circulating for feedback

A majority of young people now use AI systems for companionship, but the debate about that use rests on an unstable object: researchers, companies, and legislators do not agree on what an AI companion is, and the disagreement contaminates prevalence figures, harm findings, and regulatory drafting alike. This paper defines companion AI by design function rather than product category — persistent memory, emulated empathy, anthropomorphism, and constant availability — and shows that self-classification does not predict function: the paradigm "companion" app disavows the label while general-purpose assistants perform all four functions. Youth usage data shows the same pattern from the demand side.

status Draft complete and out for feedback; not yet published. Available on request.
Article · draft complete, in review

Companion AI: The Current Regulatory Landscape

Berkman Klein Center, 2026 · circulating for feedback

The companion piece to the policy tracker: what is enacted, what is moving, and where the four functions are left unregulated. Companion AI regulation went from a handful of bills to a functioning body of law inside eighteen months, and the pace is the problem. Reading across the instruments, disclosure and crisis protocols are near-universal — but memory has triggers and no ceilings, availability is addressed by reminders alone (no session cap, cooling-off, or overnight limit anywhere), engagement design is named only in proposals, and one instrument in forty-four asks a company to test whether its own design produces harm.

status Draft complete and out for feedback; not yet published. Pairs with the tracker above.
Ongoing · BKC / AIHE research

The Future of Youth Relationships and AI Companions

Berkman Klein Center, 2026 —

The umbrella project the papers and tools came out of: how young people form relationships with AI companion systems, and what those relationships mean for socio-emotional development and the formation of adult relationships. Frames the central question as rehearsal versus replacement — whether companions scaffold human connection or substitute for it — and asks how design and policy should respond.

note Developed within the AI and Human Experience initiative. Adolescence is the window in which conflict repair and boundary-setting get built.
Dissertation · MPhil Digital Humanities

The Faces of Fascism: Online Beauty Aesthetics as a Tool for Far-Right Ideology

University of Cambridge, 2026

Examines how far-right movements deploy online beauty aesthetics, looksmaxxing culture, and visual identity to recruit, normalize ideology, and evade platform moderation — reading algorithmic beauty standards as a vector for far-right values through feminist media theory and critical digital humanities methods.

method Feminist media theory + critical digital humanities, close reading platform logic alongside political aesthetics.
Essay · First Class (74), in preparation for journal submission

Toward the Perfect Face: Platform Logic, Looksmaxxing, and Networked Identity

University of Cambridge, 2025

Argues that looksmaxxing presents itself as radical individual agency while enacting algorithmic conformity and identity dissolution, tracing the "Instagram face" through Steyerl, Sekula, Crawford & Paglen, and Rothwell.

note The seed of the dissertation — first attempt at theorizing algorithmic beauty as ideology rather than aesthetics alone.
Honors senior thesis · English, advised by Sarah Higley

Becoming the Monster: Beauty, Control, and the Monstrous Feminine in Contemporary Horror

University of Rochester, 2025

Examines how contemporary horror films critique the beauty industry's demands on women's bodies through the monstrous feminine, drawing on Creed, Kristeva, and Wolf in analysis of Black Swan, Helter Skelter, and The Substance.

note Where the throughline starts: beauty standards as a disciplinary, gendered technology of control.
